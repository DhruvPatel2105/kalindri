Where the task weights come from

Estimated using Pearson's published question counts and scoring rubrics. Pearson does not publish task weightings. This is our derivation, not an official figure.

We count how many questions of each type appear in the exam and how many rubric marks each one carries, then work out what share of a skill score that type can move. Where a task feeds two skills — Read Aloud scores into both Reading and Speaking — its marks are counted in both.

The CLB conversion is official

The PTE Core score to Canadian Language Benchmark alignment table is published by Pearson and used by IRCC for Express Entry, and we use it unmodified. This is the most reliable figure on the page, and it is why your CLB band is shown before any number. Your benchmark is set by your lowest single skill — not an average — which is why one skill can hold the other three back.

The range is an approximation

No official or peer-reviewed conversion exists from rubric marks to the 10–90 scale. The range you see is our approximation from your rubric-level scores, shown as a range because a single number would fake a precision we do not have.

This is not a predicted exam result. It describes the work you have done here, not what you will score on test day. Treat the CLB band as the signal and the range as a rough position inside it.

Why some figures say “needs more data”

Below five attempts on a task type, your accuracy swings too much to rank or estimate from, so we say so rather than showing a number that would move next week.
