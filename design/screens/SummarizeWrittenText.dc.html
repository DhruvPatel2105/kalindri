Canada's foreign credential recognition system has long been criticised for its fragmentation. Because licensing sits with provincial regulators rather than the federal government, an engineer accredited in Ontario may still face a further assessment when moving to Alberta, and an internationally trained nurse can spend two years in bridging programs before practising at all.

Recent federal funding has attempted to shorten these timelines by paying for assessment costs directly and by encouraging regulators to accept common evidence standards. Early evaluations suggest the money reduces individual expense more reliably than it reduces waiting time, because the bottleneck is assessor capacity rather than applicant funding. Analysts therefore argue that credential recognition is a labour-supply problem inside the regulators themselves, not an information problem among newcomers.
